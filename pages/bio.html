Bio-Inspired Robot Gripper.

A two-finger end effector for an ABB arm handling a payload that deforms if you grip it too hard. FEM validated, topology optimized to drop 40% of its mass, and simulated through a full pick-and-place cycle in RobotStudio.

The brief.

Design a two-finger end effector for an ABB industrial arm handling cake on a production line, and for furnace loading. Two jobs at opposite ends of the same problem: one payload deforms if you hold it too hard, the other sits in an environment that punishes anything heavier than it needs to be.

It came out of a 15-day exchange at Ural Federal University. The first week was factory visits, ABB RobotStudio training, and learning Russian CAD. The design was the second week, which is a real constraint on a design and worth saying plainly.

// two-finger end effector · FEM validated · topology optimized · ABB RobotStudio

Designing for a deformable payload.

A gripper for rigid parts is a force problem: clamp hard enough and friction does the rest. Cake inverts that. The grip has to be firm enough that the payload does not slip out mid-transfer, and gentle enough that it arrives intact, and those two bounds are close together.

That pushed the design toward a bio-inspired two-finger geometry that distributes contact over an area instead of concentrating it at a point, so the holding force needed for a secure grip stays under the force that damages the payload.

FEM & topology optimization.

I modelled the gripper in Fusion 360, then took it into Kompas 3D for the optimization work.

  • FEM analysis on the fingers and mounting structure to check they hold up under grip and inertial loads, and to find where material was carrying load versus just being present.
  • Topology optimization driven by those results, removing 40% of the mass while keeping the stiffness that matters at the fingertips.
  • Re-checked the optimized geometry under FEM rather than trusting the optimizer's output as-is.

Mass matters more on an end effector than almost anywhere else on a robot. Every gram at the wrist is carried by every joint upstream and paid for in cycle time, so 40% off the tool is worth more than 40% off a base casting.

// 40% mass reduction · FEM validated before and after

Simulating the line.

A gripper that works on the bench can still be wrong for the cell. I modelled the assembly and the pick-and-place cycle in ABB RobotStudio, planning the arm's motion to check reach, approach angles, and clearance against the surrounding equipment before committing to the geometry.
